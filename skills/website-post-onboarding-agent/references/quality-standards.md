# Quality Standards for Website Agent Output

## Market Research Findings

### Good Research Meets These Standards:
- ✅ **Specific, not generic**: "Home service businesses in the gutter cleaning sector face 40% customer acquisition cost increases due to seasonal demand" (specific) vs. "Home services are competitive" (generic)
- ✅ **Data-backed**: All claims have sources (Google Trends, analyst reports, SERP analysis)
- ✅ **Actionable insights**: Research identifies what the client should do differently, not just what the market looks like
- ✅ **Competitor analysis covers 5-7 direct competitors**: Not adjacent services or massive aggregators
- ✅ **Competitive matrix is clear and sortable**: Can quickly see who competes on what dimension
- ✅ **Positioning gaps identified**: Report explicitly states where competitors cluster and where whitespace exists

### Red Flags:
- ❌ Generic statements like "There's a lot of competition"
- ❌ Fewer than 5 competitors analyzed
- ❌ Competitor analysis that's too shallow (just homepage screenshots)
- ❌ No actionable insights for positioning
- ❌ Missing market size/growth data

## Positioning Strategy

### Good Positioning Is:
- ✅ **Defensible**: Based on market research, not just gut feel
- ✅ **Specific**: Targets a defined segment with a clear pain point
- ✅ **Differentiated**: Avoids copying competitor messaging
- ✅ **Believable**: Client can actually deliver on the promise
- ✅ **Measurable**: Can track success with KPIs

### Positioning Statement Template:
```
For [target segment] 
who [pain point], 
[company name] is [solution] 
that [key benefit] 
because [differentiation/proof].
```

**Example** (Good):
"For homeowners who want gutter cleaning done right the first time without scheduling hassles, Clean Cut Gutters is the gutter service that guarantees zero-clog guarantees on first clean because we use proprietary inspection technology and same-day service."

**Example** (Bad):
"We're the best gutter cleaning company."

### Positioning Validation:
1. **Does research support this?** Can you point to 2-3 data points backing the claim?
2. **Can competitors claim this too?** If yes, it's not differentiated enough.
3. **Is it narrow enough?** Good positioning feels specific to a segment.
4. **Can the client deliver?** Talk to them—they should feel confident about this position.

## Website Brief

### Good Brief Meets These Standards:
- ✅ **Concise**: ~300-400 words when formatted (not bloated)
- ✅ **Structured**: Clear sections (summary, messaging pillars, voice, homepage structure, KPIs)
- ✅ **Actionable**: Design team can understand what to build without asking questions
- ✅ **Messaging is consistent**: All pillars support the positioning statement
- ✅ **CTAs are clear**: Primary and secondary CTAs are defined and justified
- ✅ **KPIs are specific**: Not "increase leads" but "increase qualified leads by 30%"

### Brief Checklist:
- [ ] Executive summary explains the positioning (1 sentence)
- [ ] Messaging pillars (3-4) all support the positioning
- [ ] Voice/tone description helps copywriter/designer make style decisions
- [ ] Homepage structure is specific (e.g., "Hero → Value Prop 1 → Case Study → Value Prop 2 → Risk Reversal CTA")
- [ ] Primary CTA is named and justified
- [ ] Secondary CTA is named and justified
- [ ] KPIs are SMART (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound)
- [ ] No design decisions in the brief (brief is strategic, not visual)

### Red Flags:
- ❌ Brief is over 500 words
- ❌ Messaging pillars are vague ("be the best")
- ❌ No clear CTAs
- ❌ KPIs like "improve SEO" (too vague)
- ❌ No connection to positioning research

## Homepage Copy

### Good Copy Meets These Standards:
- ✅ **Conversion-focused**: Talks about customer benefit, not features
- ✅ **Specific**: Uses concrete language, not buzzwords
- ✅ **Benefit-driven**: "Never clean gutters again" vs. "Gutter cleaning services"
- ✅ **CTA-clear**: Primary action is obvious
- ✅ **Social proof section included**: Case study, testimonial, or guarantee
- ✅ **Follows brand voice**: Tone matches positioning + brief recommendation
- ✅ **Scannable**: Short paragraphs, strong subheadings

### Copy Structure Checklist:
- [ ] **Hero Section**: 
  - Headline speaks to primary pain point or desired outcome
  - Subheadline reinforces benefit or proof
  - Primary CTA is visible and clear
- [ ] **Value Proposition Section** (3-4 blocks):
  - Each block leads with benefit (not feature)
  - Supporting copy is 1-2 sentences max
  - Icon/image supports the benefit
- [ ] **Social Proof Section**:
  - Testimonial, case study, guarantee, or credential
  - Proof is specific and believable
- [ ] **Final CTA**:
  - Reinforces primary action
  - Clear next step for visitor

### Copy Quality Check:
- ✅ Read aloud—does it sound like a real person talking?
- ✅ Highlight every benefit—does copy focus on benefits, not features?
- ✅ Remove buzzwords—can you replace them with concrete language?
- ✅ Check CTAs—are they action-oriented and clear?

### Red Flags:
- ❌ Buzzwords like "innovative," "cutting-edge," "synergy"
- ❌ Focus on features, not benefits ("We use advanced technology" vs. "You save 5 hours per month")
- ❌ No clear primary CTA
- ❌ Social proof section missing
- ❌ Copy is too long (more than 2 sentences per section)
- ❌ Tone doesn't match brand voice recommendation

## General Output Quality

### For All Deliverables:
- ✅ **No typos or grammar errors**: Proofread before submission
- ✅ **Consistent terminology**: Use "customer," "client," "homeowner" etc. consistently
- ✅ **Links work**: Any links referenced are valid
- ✅ **Formatting is clean**: Clear hierarchy, scannable sections
- ✅ **Client-ready**: Should look professional to show to the client

---

## How to Evaluate Output

### Quick 5-Minute Check:
1. Is research specific and data-backed? (skim for concrete numbers)
2. Is positioning statement clear and different from competitors? (read the positioning statement)
3. Is brief concise and actionable? (count words, check structure)
4. Is copy benefit-focused and conversion-ready? (read hero section)
5. Are CTAs clear? (can you identify what action the visitor should take?)

### Deeper 30-Minute Review:
1. Read market research for gaps—are there 5-7 competitors? Is the gap analysis clear?
2. Review positioning against research—does the evidence support it?
3. Check messaging pillars—do all 4 relate to the positioning?
4. Read copy aloud—does it sound natural? Are benefits clear?
5. Check KPIs—are they SMART and measurable?

### Feedback Loop:
If output doesn't meet standards:
1. Note specific issues (e.g., "Competitor analysis only covers 3 competitors, need 5-7")
2. Reference the quality standard (link to this doc)
3. Ask agent to revise specific section
4. Re-check against standard before approval
